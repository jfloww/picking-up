# English corrections — 2026-08-28

1. You wrote: "I want to fix a bug that click action in the weekly tab."
   Better: "I want to fix a bug in the weekly tab's click behavior."
   Why: "a bug that ..." starts a relative clause, so it needs a verb ("a bug that breaks the click action"). If you just want to point at where the bug is, use a preposition instead: "a bug in the click action."

2. You wrote: "I hope if I click the card of the text, i want to see the details"
   Better: "I'd like to see the details when I click anywhere on the card."
   Why: "I hope ..." and "I want ..." are two separate main clauses stacked on one sentence — keep one. Also "the card of the text" is unclear; the card *contains* the text, so "the card" (or "anywhere on the card") is what you mean.

3. You wrote: "currently I think it should be clicked the title line?"
   Better: "currently I think you have to click the title line."
   Why: "it should be clicked the title line" mixes passive ("be clicked") with an object ("the title line"), which passive voice can't take. Active voice is clearer here: "you have to click the title line."

4. You wrote: "can you fix the bug and make a pr"
   Better: "Could you fix the bug and open a PR?"
   Why: mostly fine — "open a PR" is the more common phrasing than "make a PR," and "PR" is usually capitalized.
